Jail Time Records

From the festival

Traveling through an overcrowded prison labyrinth in Douala, Cameroon, you’ll find a mercurial village full of petty thieves, murderers, and political scapegoats. A New Bell Prison ‘barker’ winds us through a chaotic din of bullies, hagglers, and sleepers, past guards pumping iron, into a closet-sized sanctuary where something magical happens. This is Jail Time Records, a music label founded by the film’s co-directors, former prisoner Steve Happi and his partner, Dione Roach. What began as an in-house recording studio is now a vital platform for artists—both incarcerated and free—using music to forge second chances amid poverty, corruption, and an overburdened judicial system. This directorial debut is unlike most prison documentaries, moving between the grit of daily survival and sudden bursts of rap, song, and dance, creating a shared stage of extraordinary expression. The label may not be able to engineer freedom from systemic failure, but it does offer hope for redemption and a different future for those who want it. Winner of three top prizes at the 2026 Tribeca Film Festival: Best Documentary Feature, Albert Maysles Award for Best New Documentary, and Best Cinematography in a Documentary Feature - Michelle Svenson
